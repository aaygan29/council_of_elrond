# The Empirical Gate Ladder - full definitions

Each gate is a question the work must survive. For each, this file gives: the
question, the specific checks, what a FAIL looks like, and a worked example drawn
from real in-silico studies so the standard is concrete rather than abstract.

Run the gates in order. Gate 0 is terminal - if data integrity fails, downstream
gates are moot. Mark each PASS / FAIL / N/A with concrete evidence.

---

## Gate 0 - Provenance & Integrity

**Question:** Can the findings even be evaluated, or is the substrate broken?

**Checks:**
- Is the data real, or a synthetic/placeholder stand-in being treated as real?
- Train/test leakage: are test items, subjects, or time windows touched during
  training, feature selection, or hyperparameter tuning?
- Is the split fixed and pre-declared, or does it move between analyses?
- Is there a **noise ceiling** (for encoding/RSA) or an honest **chance baseline**
  (for classification/fingerprinting)? A score is meaningless without the ceiling
  it should be read against.
- Provenance hygiene: is the "authoritative" results file clearly distinguished
  from pilots/archives? Are deprecated artifacts marked not-for-citation?

**FAIL looks like:** synthetic data presented as empirical; test-set tuning;
a fingerprinting/decoding result with no chance baseline; a headline number whose
source file can't be located.

**Worked example (the DO_NOT_CITE lesson):** A pilot reported near-perfect
subject fingerprinting. But "subjects" were generated by different random number
generators, so the classifier was separating RNG seeds, not biology. Encoding
accuracy was ~chance (r ~ 0.006) while fingerprinting was trivially perfect - a
contradiction that only makes sense if the individuation signal is an artifact.
Correct handling: archive as synthetic_pilot_DO_NOT_CITE, never compare against
it. A Gate 0 failure forces REJECT regardless of how elegant the method is.

## Gate 1 - Variance / Replication

**Question:** Is the headline a stable effect or a lucky run?

**Checks:**
- Single run vs. multiple seeds. One seed is an anecdote, not a result.
- Is an **effect size** reported (Cohen's d, rho, R^2, delta), not just a p-value?
- Is a **confidence interval** (preferably bootstrap) given for the headline?
- For "improvement" claims: is the improvement larger than the seed-to-seed
  standard deviation of the baseline?
- Was the comparison pre-committed, or chosen after peeking?

**FAIL looks like:** "Our method improved bpb from 4.39 to 4.37" with N=1 and no
spread; a p < .05 with no effect size; a CI that comfortably includes the null.

**Worked example (the seed-variance kill):** A single-run result showed a
small-world regularizer improving a language model. Re-run across 20 seeds x 2
conditions with a Welch t-test and bootstrap CI, the regularizer in fact hurt
performance (delta = +0.066 bpb, Cohen's d = -1.20, p = 0.9997 against the
improvement hypothesis). The original "improvement" was entirely seed variance.
A single-run headline is at most T1; demand multi-seed → MAJOR REVISION.

## Gate 2 - Specification Robustness

**Question:** Does the result survive reasonable, defensible analytic choices, or
does it live on a knife-edge?

**Checks:**
- Threshold / hyperparameter sensitivity: does the finding hold across a
  reasonable sweep, or only at one hand-picked value?
- Preprocessing forks: would a standard alternative preserve the effect?
- Are specific sub-claims (e.g., "layer 3 is the peak") as robust as the
  directional claim ("early layers differ from late")?

**FAIL looks like:** an effect significant at exactly one threshold; a peak-layer
or peak-ROI claim that moves every time the density cutoff changes.

**Worked example:** An early/late topological split was tested at 7 density
thresholds. The directional split was significant at 4/7 - a real but partial
effect. But the specific "layer 3 is the peak" claim held at 0/7 thresholds.
Correct handling: keep the robust directional claim, drop the unstable point estimate.

---

## Gate 3 - Specificity

**Question:** Is the named mechanism specifically responsible, or would any
generic intervention of equal strength produce the same effect?

**Checks:**
- Is there an ablation/control condition isolating the named mechanism from
  generic alternatives of matched strength?
- For "X-like structure helps" claims: do non-X structures of the same magnitude
  help equally? If so, the benefit is from the intervention's strength, not its X-ness.
- Multiple-comparison discipline across the condition set (ANOVA + corrected pairwise).

**FAIL looks like:** "Brain-like topology improves the model" with no comparison
to non-brain-like topologies of equal regularization strength.

**Worked example:** The crux experiment ran 6 topology conditions x 20 seeds with
one-way ANOVA + Holm-Bonferroni pairwise tests. Ranking: none > lattice >
small_world > degenerate > random > scale_free -- no topology regularizer beat the
unregularized baseline, and the "special" one was mid-pack.

---

## Gate 4 - Confound Control

**Question:** Does the effect survive controlling for the obvious confound?

**Checks:**
- Name the top 1-3 confounds for this design (model/sample size, sequence
  length, surprisal/frequency, base rate, acquisition site).
- Is there a partial correlation / covariate-adjusted version of the headline?
- Does the raw effect shrink to non-significance once the confound is partialled out?

**FAIL looks like:** a cross-model or cross-subject correlation reported raw, with
no control for the variable that trivially co-varies with both axes (almost always size).

**Worked example:** "Better models are more brain-like" was supported by a raw
correlation (Spearman rho = -0.83 between early-layer brain-similarity and
log-perplexity, p = 0.042). But controlling for log(parameter count), the partial
correlation fell to rho = -0.67, p = 0.143 - not significant. The relationship
was substantially scale-driven. Demand the partial correlation; if the effect
dies, the causal-sounding claim must be retracted to "size-confounded association."

---

## Gate 5 - Mechanism / Necessity

**Question:** Does the proposed driver actually carry the functional load, or is
it present-but-redundant?

**Checks:**
- Is there a necessity test - ablation, lesion, knockout - showing the proposed
  component is required for the outcome?
- Compare the functional load of the named component against the components doing
  the real work. "Present" and "load-bearing" are different claims.
- For trajectory/emergence claims: is the property a cause or a byproduct?

**FAIL looks like:** "These brain-like heads are important" with no ablation.

**Worked example:** Ablating the top-10 brain-like attention heads cost +0.031
bpb; ablating the top-10 non-brain-like heads cost +0.264 bpb - an 8.6x
functional-load ratio. The brain-like heads were present but redundant. The honest
conclusion flipped from "brain-like structure is functional" to "brain-like
structure is an emergent byproduct, not a performance driver."

---

## Gate 6 - Claim Calibration

**Question:** Does every claim's language match its evidence tier, and does the
narrative survive a hostile retelling?

**Checks:**
- Tier-language match: causal verbs ("causes", "drives", "enables") require
  necessity/intervention evidence (T5), not correlation (T3-T4).
- **In-silico is not in-vivo.** A result in a model is evidence about the model.
  Flag every silent jump from "in our network" to "in the brain."
- **Fact/scenario blur.** Distinguish demonstrated results from illustrative
  scenarios, projections, or threat models.
- Abstract-vs-results consistency: does the abstract promise what the results deliver?
- **Garden of forking paths:** were analytic choices pre-registered, or selected
  after seeing the data?
- **Hostile retelling:** restate the central finding assuming the least flattering
  valid interpretation. If the contribution evaporates, the framing is overclaiming.

**FAIL looks like:** an abstract that says "induces brain-like processing" when the
results show a non-significant RSA alignment (rho = 0.03, p = 0.48, 0/48 tests
surviving correction).

---

## Cross-cutting statistical standards

- **Effect size + CI > p-value.** A p alone is nearly uninformative.
- **Multiple comparisons.** Many tests → correction (Holm-Bonferroni, FDR).
- **Permutation / non-parametric** tests for small-N or non-normal data.
- **Bootstrap CIs** for anything without a clean analytic interval.
- **Noise ceiling / chance baseline** as the denominator of meaning.
- **N and power.** Small N + null = "underpowered", not "no effect".
